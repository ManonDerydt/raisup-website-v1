# Structural Shifts 2026–2030 (AI + Demographics) Creating New Mass Software Needs

Research date: 2026-09-28. ~20 tool calls (web search snippets; few full-page fetches, so figures come from search-result summaries of the cited pages — verify exact numbers against primary PDFs before publishing). US-centric data dominates; global/UK/EU equivalents were not researched in depth (gap).

## 1. Labor market disruption by AI: displaced white-collar workers, entry-level collapse, reskilling, proving human skills

### Takeaway
Hard 2026 evidence now shows AI concentrated on *hiring freezes for young workers in AI-exposed jobs* (−19% relative employment gap, Stanford/ADP Aug 2026) and AI becoming the #1 self-reported layoff reason in the US for several consecutive months of 2026 (Challenger). Needs: career transition/job-search copilots for displaced mid-career workers, "proof of human/tacit skill" credentials, and entry-level on-ramps (portfolio/apprenticeship substitutes). Enterprise assessment/reskilling is crowded; consumer-side self-serve "prove what I can do" and "pivot plan" tools are much less so.

### Cited Findings
- Stanford Digital Economy Lab "Canaries in the Coal Mine" August 2026 update: employment of workers aged 22–25 in highly AI-exposed occupations is ~19% below trend vs. less-exposed peers, up from 15% a year earlier; decline driven mainly by reduced hiring, not separations; concentrated in "codified knowledge" roles while experienced workers (tacit knowledge) show no comparable decline; based on ADP payroll data covering 26M+ US workers through June 2026; headline: "No widespread displacement" — [Stanford DEL news, Aug 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/); [Paper PDF](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf); [Fortune, 2026-06-27](https://fortune.com/2026/06/27/what-is-ai-impact-entry-level-jobs-stanford-adp-canaries-brynjolfsson-richardson/)
- Stanford DEL also published (Feb 9, 2026) analysis on interest rates/timing as alternative drivers of young-worker employment changes — i.e., causality is still debated — [Stanford DEL, 2026-02-09](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canaries-interest-rates-and-timinga-more-on-recent-drivers-of-employment-changes-for-young-workers)
- Challenger, Gray & Christmas: AI led all stated reasons for US job cuts for the third straight month in May 2026 with 38,579 cuts (40% of May cuts, highest monthly total since tracking began in 2023); YTD 2026 AI cited in 87,714 cuts (22% of all 2026 layoffs), already above the 54,836 for all of 2025; 173,568 cumulative since 2023, ~59% of it in H1 2026 — [Challenger May 2026 report PDF](https://www.challengergray.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Challenger-Report-May-2026.pdf); [CNBC, 2026-06-05](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/05/ai-is-now-the-leading-reason-companies-give-for-cutting-jobs-says-new-report-what-that-means-for-workers.html). A later Challenger post headlines "AI leads for fifth straight month" (i.e., through ~July 2026) — [Challenger blog](https://www.challengergray.com/blog/challenger-report-layoffs-fall-hiring-picks-up-ai-leads-for-fifth-straight-month/)
- AI accounted for 26% of April 2026 job cuts — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ai-layoffs-job-cuts-challenger-report-april-2026/)
- Tech sector: 139,156 US job cuts announced H1 2026, +83% vs 76,214 in H1 2025 (~1/3 of all US cuts) — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/tech-layoffs-surge-83percent-h1-2026-challenger-ai-disruption/824320/)
- Caveat: experts warn companies may be "scapegoating" AI for cuts driven by other factors — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/05/ai-is-now-the-leading-reason-companies-give-for-cutting-jobs-says-new-report-what-that-means-for-workers.html)
- Recent-grad unemployment (ages 22–27) rose to 5.8% at end of 2025 (NY Fed), above the 4.3% national rate — historically grads did better than average; 35% of entry-level jobs require AI skills — [CNBC Select](https://www.cnbc.com/select/class-of-2026-hiring-stats-and-ai-trends/)
- NACE: employers' Class of 2026 hiring projection revised from +1.6% (fall) to +5.6% (spring update); 45% of employers rate the market "fair" — [NACE press 2026](https://www.naceweb.org/about-us/press/2026/outlook-brightens-for-college-class-of-2026-entry-level-hiring); [NACE Spring Update](https://www.naceweb.org/research/reports/2026/job-outlook/spring-update/) (note: contrasts with Stanford/NY Fed signals — the entry-level picture is mixed, not a uniform collapse)
- WEF Future of Jobs 2025 (Jan 2025): 170M jobs created and 92M displaced by 2030 (net +78M), 22% of jobs disrupted; 39% of key skills to change by 2030; 63% of employers cite skills gaps as top barrier; survey of 1,000+ employers / 14M+ workers — [WEF press release](https://www.weforum.org/press/2025/01/future-of-jobs-report-2025-78-million-new-job-opportunities-by-2030-but-urgent-upskilling-needed-to-prepare-workforces/); [Report PDF](https://reports.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Future_of_Jobs_Report_2025.pdf)
- Anthropic Economic Index: on Claude.ai, augmentation rose to 52% vs automation 45% (Nov 2025 data), but enterprise API use is ~75% automation; coding tasks migrating from augmentative chat to automated API workflows; subsequent reports March 2026 ("Learning curves") and June 2026 ("Cadences") — [Jan 2026 report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report); [Mar 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report); [Jun 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report)
- Skills-verification startups exist but are small/B2B, e.g., Proof of Skill (video challenges for candidates; $250K from Antler, Dec 2024) — [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/proofofskill/__pYYt2UyAVY8vA9dp9mYU_REFArJIpJZEDFMA4XGs7Bc/funding-and-investors); commentary that AI "removed the last excuse for relying on traditional credentials" leaving employers with a measurement problem — [CXM](https://cxm.world/employee-experience/skills-based-hiring-ai-era-credentialism/)

### Inferences
- The Stanford finding (codified vs tacit knowledge) suggests a product thesis: tools that let workers *demonstrate tacit judgment* (work samples, recorded decision-making, verified references, "AI-assisted but human-directed" portfolios) rather than codified knowledge tests that AI can pass.
- Displaced mid-career white-collar workers (tens of thousands/month cited AI cuts in the US) need a self-serve "transition OS": severance/benefits/COBRA admin, financial runway calculator, pivot-to-freelance setup, AI-proof skill mapping. Outplacement is enterprise-sold (Challenger itself, LHH); a consumer-paid, viral version is white space. Likely acquirers: LinkedIn, Indeed/Glassdoor (Recruit), Coursera, Upwork/Fiverr.
- Entry-level: substitute for the vanishing "first job" — AI-supervised apprenticeship/simulated work projects that yield verifiable evidence. Handshake is the obvious acquirer/competitor.

- No non-US (UK/EU/India) displacement data collected.
- No funding data found for consumer-facing "career transition copilot" startups; landscape uncertain.

## 2. One-person company / solopreneur explosion: missing infrastructure

### Takeaway
Solo founding hit record highs in 2026 (63% of Stripe Atlas C-corps in Q2 2026; 19% of a YC batch), and the US independent workforce is ~73M (27.6M full-time). Revenue dispersion among solo founders is widening (top decile 61x median). Needs: agent-run back office (bookkeeping, tax, compliance), portable benefits/insurance, trust/reputation for tiny vendors, and managing their own agent stack.

### Cited Findings
- Solo founders = 63% of C-corps formed via Stripe Atlas in Q2 2026 (all-time high); among solo-founded Atlas startups, median first-six-month revenue in 2025 fell 23% YoY while top-decile rose 19%; top decile earned 61x the median (vs 34x four years earlier) — [Stripe blog](https://stripe.com/blog/top-solo-founder-traits); [Stripe on X](https://x.com/stripe/status/2060050284051620336)
- Carta: solo founders were 36% of startups incorporated on its platform in 2025, roughly double a decade earlier; ~19% of YC Spring 2026 batch (190+ companies) solo-founded (independent review) — [Linas Substack](https://linas.substack.com/p/one-person-company-ai-agents); [Runtimewire](https://runtimewire.com/article/yc-solo-founders-startups-bits-atoms-2026) (secondary sources)
- MBO Partners 2025: 72.9M US independent workers (vs 72.7M in 2024); full-time independents flat at 27.6M; occasional independents 37.4M; part-time fell to 7.9M (from 11.1M in 2022); 11.5M independent professional service providers working with businesses — [MBO Partners 2025](https://www.mbopartners.com/state-of-independence); [SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/number-of-independent-workers-rises-again-mbo-partners)
- Claims that a solo operator's AI tool stack costs $200–500/month vs. salaries; "AI agents handle 80–85% of execution" — [BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/one-person-unicorn-ai-agents-solo-founder-billion-dollar-2026) (vendor/blog claim, low reliability)
- Formation market for non-residents is already competitive: Stripe Atlas, Firstbase, Doola, Bizee — [GlobalSolo comparison 2026](https://www.globalsolo.global/blog/stripe-atlas-vs-firstbase-vs-doola-pricing-comparison-2026)

### Inferences
- Note the tension: MBO data shows the *headcount* of full-time independents is flat, while *company formation* by solo founders is surging. The boom is in solo *companies* (incorporated, software/AI-leveraged), not necessarily in gig workers. Product should target incorporated solo founders/micro-SaaS/agencies globally.
- Formation is crowded; the post-formation layer is not: "autopilot compliance" (annual reports, sales tax/VAT across countries, 1099/DAC7, contracts), cross-border payroll-free benefits (health, disability, retirement for solo founders outside US), and "trust signals" for one-person vendors selling to enterprises (security questionnaires, SOC2-lite, insurance certificates auto-generated). Strategic acquirers: Stripe (Atlas), Gusto, Deel, Shopify, Intuit, Rippling, Mercury.
- Widening revenue dispersion (61x) suggests demand for distribution/growth tools for the median solo founder who is falling behind — but this is crowded (marketing tools).

## 3. SMBs managing a workforce of agents: cost control, accountability

### Takeaway
SMB agent spend is rising fast but governance/observability tooling targets enterprises; analysts flag "AI-nomics"/FinOps for AI as a must-have for mid-market. A self-serve "agent payroll/HR" layer (budget caps, logs, who-did-what, approval rules, cross-vendor bill) for 5–200 person companies appears under-served, but evidence of demand is mostly from vendor/analyst blogs.

### Cited Findings
- AI agent spending projected $86.4B (2025) → ~$206.5B (2026), +139% (forecast, secondary source citing analyst estimate) — [IV Consulting](https://ivconsulting.in/blogs/ai-agent-spending-boom-what-it-means-for-small-businesses/)
- Most SMBs spend $500–5,000/month on off-the-shelf AI; custom agents $30K–100K upfront; teams monitoring usage in real time cut waste ~30%, but many SMBs lack observability to detect runaway spend; Techaisle 2026 SMB predictions call "AI-nomics" (tracking/allocating/capping inference, API, storage costs) non-negotiable for mid-market — [Search summary of Reinventing.ai / Techaisle-related sources](https://insights.reinventing.ai/articles/ai-agents-cost-performance-smb-2026-04-08) (low-to-medium reliability; estimates)
- Upwork published a "State of AI within SMBs in 2026" report — [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/resources/state-of-ai-in-smbs) (content not fetched)

### Inferences
- Enterprise agent governance (identity, observability) is being built by large vendors (Microsoft, Okta, Salesforce, Datadog, etc. — not verified in this session). The SMB version—a "Gusto for agents": onboard an agent, give it a budget/card, permissions, a manager, performance review, and offboarding—is a clear positioning with obvious acquirers (Gusto, Rippling, Ramp/Brex for spend, Intuit).
- Accountability angle: when an agent makes a mistake with a customer, SMBs need audit trails usable for disputes/insurance — ties into emerging AI liability insurance (not researched).

## 4. Trust crisis: AI-enabled fraud, deepfakes, voice clones

### Takeaway
Reported fraud hit records in 2025 (FBI IC3 $20.9B; FTC $15.9B), and the FBI added its first dedicated AI section ($893M AI-tagged losses, surely undercounted). Detection startups are funded but B2B/enterprise (Reality Defender, GetReal, Pindrop). Consumer/family/SMB self-serve protection (family verification, elder-parent scam shield, SMB payment-verification for invoice/voice fraud) is comparatively open, with strong acquirers (Aura/Norton-Gen Digital/McAfee, banks, telcos).

### Cited Findings
- FBI IC3 2025 Annual Report: $20.877B total reported losses, >1M complaints — first time above $20B; first-ever dedicated AI section: 22,364 AI-related complaints, $893M adjusted losses (investment fraud $632M, BEC $30M, tech support $19.5M); deepfake/voice spoofing in online job interviews ~$13M — [IC3 2025 report PDF](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf); [SecureWorld](https://www.secureworld.io/industry-news/ai-enabled-fraud-topped-893m-fbi); [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/fbi-ftc-report-2025-losses/)
- ~85% of cybercrime losses in 2025 came from social engineering (per analysis of IC3 data) — [Ninjio](https://ninjio.com/blog/fbi-why-85-of-cybercrime-losses-in-2025-point-to-a-human-problem/) (vendor analysis)
- FTC Consumer Sentinel: consumers reported $15.9B fraud losses in 2025 (vs >$12B in 2024), ~3M fraud reports (vs 2.6M); imposter scams >1M reports and $3.5B; FTC estimated total 2024 cost of fraud incl. underreporting could be as high as $195.9B — [FTC JEC testimony, 2026-03-25](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/ftc-testimony-jec-hearing-on-the-rising-scam-economy.pdf); [FTC press release, June 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/06/ftc-data-show-people-reported-losing-3-point-5-billion-imposter-scams-2025); [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/03/269398-us-ftc-reports-15-9-billion-in-consumer-fraud-losses-in-past-year/)
- Deepfake detection funding (B2B): Reality Defender $33M Series A (total ~$52.4M), GetReal Security $17.5M Series A (Forgepoint); both "Market Shapers" in Gartner 2026 Emerging Market Quadrant for deepfake detection; Pindrop, Clarity, Truepic raised in 2024 — [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/reality-defender-banks-33m-to-tackle-ai-generated-deepfakes/); [GetReal](https://www.getrealsecurity.com/resources/getreal-security-raises-17-5m-series-a-led-by-forgepoint-capital-to-address-rapidly-growing-threats-associated-with-generative-ai-and-malicious-digital-media); [Tech-Insider](https://tech-insider.org/reality-defender-vs-hive-vs-sensity-2026/)
- YC has a "deepfake detection" industry tag with multiple funded startups — [YC directory](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/industry/deepfake-detection)
- Consumer advice currently relies on manual measures like family "safe words" — [Total Defense](https://www.totaldefense.com/security-blog/how-consumers-can-protect-themselves-from-ai-scams-deepfakes-and-voice-cloning/); executive/family digital-footprint reduction (VanishID) targets executives — [VanishID](https://vanishid.com/resources/blog/ai-voice-cloning-deepfake-protection/)

### Inferences
- Searching for 2026 consumer deepfake-protection startup rounds returned no specific raise — signal that consumer/family self-serve is less crowded than enterprise detection (absence of evidence, treat cautiously).
- Viral self-serve ideas: family "verification circle" (out-of-band challenge for urgent calls/money requests), scam "second opinion" for elderly parents that adult children set up (links to caregiving, section 5), SMB "verify before you pay" for invoice/voice-CEO fraud, and candidate-identity verification for small employers hiring remotely (deepfake interview fraud $13M reported).
- Acquirers: Gen Digital (Norton/LifeLock/Avast), Aura, McAfee, Experian, banks, carriers (T-Mobile/Verizon), and ID vendors (Okta, Persona, Socure).

## 5. Demographics: family caregiving, business succession ("silver tsunami"), wealth transfer

### Takeaway
63M US family caregivers (+45% in a decade) and ~6M SMBs facing ownership transition by 2035 are two of the largest, most admin-heavy mass needs. Caregiving is attracting VC (Hera $27M Series A June 2026; Homethrive; Luffu by Fitbit founders) mostly via employer/Medicare channels; succession is served by brokers/marketplaces with high failure rates (70% of listed businesses don't sell). Both have white space for self-serve, viral, family/owner-facing software.

### Cited Findings — Caregiving
- AARP/NAC "Caregiving in the US 2025" (July 2025): 63M family caregivers (~1 in 4 US adults), +45% (~20M) in a decade; 59M caring for adults; avg age 51; 3 in 5 women; 29% sandwich generation; 44% high-intensity care; 30% caregiving 5+ years; over half do medical/nursing tasks but only ~20% trained — [AARP press release 2025-07-24](https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2025-07-24-new-report-reveals-crisis-point-for-americas-63-million-family-caregivers.html); [AARP survey page](https://www.aarp.org/caregiving/basics/caregiving-in-us-survey-2025/)
- Hera raised $27M Series A (led by Bain Capital Ventures, June 2026) for virtual care coordination for families of older adults (medications, insurance navigation) — [Fortune, 2026-06-25](https://fortune.com/2026/06/25/hera-caregiving-startup-elder-care-medicare-series-a/)
- Fitbit founders (Park, Friedman) launched self-funded Luffu, an "intelligent family care system" app, later hardware — [Axios, 2026-02-03](https://www.axios.com/2026/02/03/fitbit-founders-ai-family-caregiving)
- Homethrive raised $20M for a tech-enabled caregiver platform (employer benefit) — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/homethrive-raises-20m-tech-enabled-caregiver-platform) (date not confirmed in snippet)
- Abby Care pays/trains family caregivers to become licensed paid caregivers — [Forbes, 2026-07-28](https://www.forbes.com/sites/richardnieva/2026/07/28/this-entrepreneur-will-pay-you-to-provide-home-care-for-your-loved-ones/)

### Cited Findings — Business succession
- ~6M US SMBs to face ownership transitions by 2035; >1M viable for sale, up to $5T enterprise value; first boomers turned 80 in 2026, last reach 65 in 2029 — [Forbes, 2026-02-26](https://www.forbes.com/sites/martinadilicosa/2026/02/26/millions-of-small-businesses-soon-changing-hands-as-baby-boomers-retire-in-great-ownership-transfer-report-says/); [McKinsey Institute for Economic Mobility](https://www.mckinsey.com/institute-for-economic-mobility/our-insights/the-great-ownership-transfer-a-new-era-of-business-stewardship)
- Nearly half of US small-business owners are 55+; only 54% have a succession plan — [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/small-business/millions-jobs-vulnerable-silver-tsunami-looms-us-small-businesses-experts-warn)
- Exit Planning Institute: 70% of businesses put on the market don't sell; 50% of exits are involuntary — [Patriot Growth Capital citing EPI](https://patriotgrowthcapital.com/blog/baby-boomer-business-succession) (secondary)
- BizBuySell Q2 2026: 2,117 businesses sold (−10% QoQ and YoY); $1.8B enterprise value; median sale price $349,250; cash-flow multiple 2.7x; revenue multiple 0.7x; friction from tighter credit and March 2026 SBA rule updates (citizenship, 10% equity injection/full standby) — [BizBuySell Insight Report](https://www.bizbuysell.com/insight-report/); [Sundance Financial summary](https://sundancefg.com/resources/bizbuysell-q2-2026-insight-report)

### Cited Findings — Wealth transfer
- Cerulli: $124T to transfer through 2048 ($105T to heirs, $18T to charity); ~$100T from boomers and older; Millennials inherit most ($46T); Gen X gets $14T in the next 10 years — [Cerulli press release](https://www.cerulli.com/press-releases/cerulli-anticipates-124-trillion-in-wealth-will-transfer-through-2048)
- Estimates vary widely ($36T to >$100T) and much wealth is concentrated in the top 2% of households — [CNBC, 2026-07-17](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/17/great-wealth-transfer-estimates.html)

### Inferences
- Caregiving VC money is going to employer/Medicare-reimbursed services (Hera, Homethrive). A consumer, self-serve "family command center" (shared tasks, med lists, benefits eligibility finder, bill/scam monitoring of the parent, document vault, power-of-attorney workflow) that siblings invite each other into has built-in virality (each caregiver invites 2–4 relatives). Acquirers: AARP partners, Care.com (IAC), insurers, Best Buy (formerly in this space — unverified), banks.
- Succession: the 70% no-sale rate plus tiny BizBuySell volumes (~8.5K/yr) vs >1M viable-for-sale businesses implies a massive "unprepared seller" gap. Self-serve "sale-readiness" (auto-built CIM, cleaned financials, SBA-eligibility checker, valuation), and buyer-side tools for "search fund/ETA" micro-acquirers. Acquirers: BizBuySell (CoStar), Acquire.com, Flippa, SBA lenders (Live Oak), Intuit/QuickBooks, Gusto.
- Wealth transfer: estate settlement/"death admin" for heirs is a concrete near-term need; not researched here (gap).

## 6. Energy/climate adaptation at household/SMB level

### Takeaway
US federal policy reversed in 2025: residential clean energy credits (25C heat pumps, 25D solar/batteries) ended for systems placed in service after 2025-12-31, while state-by-state IRA rebates (HOMES/HEAR) roll out unevenly — creating an incentive-navigation mess. Insurance repricing/non-renewal concentrates in high-risk ZIP codes. Needs: incentive/rebate navigators, home climate-risk + insurance shopping/mitigation tools. Evidence of startups in this session is thin.

### Cited Findings
- OBBBA (Public Law 119-21, signed 2025-07-04) terminated Section 25C early; 25C and 25D expired for systems placed in service after 2025-12-31; remaining federal paths are IRA HOMES and HEAR rebates (rolling out state by state) plus state/utility rebates — [HVAC Base](https://www.hvacbase.org/heat-pump-tax-credits-2026); [Energy Rebate Calculator blog](https://energyrebatecalculator.com/blog/heat-pump-tax-credit-2026) (secondary)
- Highest-risk ZIP codes: non-renewal rates ~80% higher than lowest-risk; avg premium $2,321 (82% higher) — [Treasury report press release](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/jy2791); average new-policy premium ~$1,950 by Dec 2025, +8.5% YoY (growth decelerating after several double-digit years) — [National Mortgage Professional](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/home-insurance-market-be-shaped-climate-risk-and-tech-26)
- Brookings mapping where climate risk and insurance costs will hit hardest — [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/where-rising-climate-risks-and-insurance-costs-will-hit-hardest/)
- Energy-cost shock: 48% of business owners (BizBuySell Q2 2026 survey) report disruptions from higher fuel/energy costs after the US–Iran conflict — [BizBuySell / Sundance summary](https://sundancefg.com/resources/bizbuysell-q2-2026-insight-report)

### Inferences
- In the US, the loss of federal credits likely *reduces* near-term heat-pump/solar demand, making this less attractive as a mass-market growth wave than in 2023–24; the stronger US need is insurance/risk (mitigation proof for discounts, e.g., fortified roofs; claims documentation). Outside the US (UK Boiler Upgrade Scheme, EU EPBD) electrification policy remains supportive — not researched here.
- Acquirers: insurers/insurtechs, Zillow/Redfin (climate risk data), Home Depot/Lowe's, utilities.

- No household climate-resilience startup funding data found.

## 7. Education disruption: parents, students and AI

### Takeaway
AI homework use is majority behavior among US teens, parents underestimate it, and schools communicate policy poorly — a parent/student "AI literacy + integrity" gap. Education is a crowded, hard-to-monetize consumer category though; B2C parent tools are the more open angle.

### Cited Findings
- Pew (survey Sept 25–Oct 9, 2025; published 2026-02-24; 1,458 teens+parents): 57% of teens use chatbots to search for info, 54% for schoolwork; 10% do all/most schoolwork with chatbot help; 64% of teens report using chatbots vs ~half of parents who think so; 76% say students at their school use chatbots to cheat at least sometimes; parents largely approve (64% for summaries, 58% homework help) — [Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/02/24/how-teens-use-and-view-ai/); [Report PDF](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/02/PI_2026.02.24_Teens-and-AI_REPORT.pdf)
- March 2026 survey (EdWeek, via summary): 47% of parents said their child's school had not provided info on its AI policy; 37% had — [GovTech / search summary](https://www.govtech.com/education/k-12/survey-finds-54-of-u-s-teens-use-ai-for-schoolwork) (secondary)
- GPTZero publishes a parent's guide to AI and schoolwork (detection vendor moving into parent audience) — [GPTZero](https://gptzero.me/news/parents-guide-to-ai-and-schoolwork/)

### Inferences
- White space: parent-facing "how is my kid using AI and are they actually learning" (process-based evidence of learning, e.g., drafts/oral checks) rather than detection. Acquirers: Chegg (weak), Khan Academy (non-profit), Google/Microsoft education, Duolingo, Instructure.

### Gaps

Ranked by (evidence strength × low crowding × self-serve/viral fit × acquirer clarity): (1) family caregiving admin + elder scam protection (63M caregivers; $15.9–20.9B reported fraud; VC flowing to B2B2C, consumer self-serve open); (2) SMB sale-readiness / succession (6M transitions, 70% no-sale; acquirers CoStar/BizBuySell, Intuit); (3) solo-company autopilot back office & trust signals (63% of Atlas C-corps solo; acquirers Stripe/Gusto/Deel); (4) SMB "agent workforce" management & spend control (thin evidence, strong acquirer logic: Gusto/Rippling/Ramp); (5) displaced/entry-level worker "proof of tacit skill" & transition tools (strong evidence, crowded enterprise side); (6) climate/insurance home tools and (7) parent AI-education tools (weaker fit or thinner evidence).
