# US local-marketing agencies as buyers of a white-label "AI bookability audit" (as of Sept 2026)

Method note: research done 2026-09-28 by web search. The egress proxy BLOCKED direct fetches of seranking.com, brightlocal.com, sparktoro.com, capconvert.com and puzzleinbox.com. Findings from those sources come from **search-engine snippets only** and are flagged [snippet]. Many "stats" pages are vendor blogs or content farms; these are flagged [vendor] or [aggregator]. Reddit / Facebook group threads did not come up in search, so community sentiment is a gap.

## 1. Size: how many agencies, how big, what they charge local clients

### Takeaway
There are about 87k to 100k US "digital advertising agencies" (IBISWorld). Most are small: most have fewer than 50 staff and many have 1 to 15. Agencies that sell to local SMBs mostly charge **$500 to $1,500/month** per client for local SEO. Many small shops carry 20 to 100+ clients at low retainers. That is exactly the "many local clients, thin margin" profile a per-client audit add-on would fit.

### Cited Findings
- US digital advertising agencies: 87,197 in 2025 (+16.3% YoY) and 100,202 in 2026 (+14.9%), per IBISWorld [snippet; IBISWorld figures are modelled estimates, and the category is broader than local-SEO shops] — [IBISWorld, 2026](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/digital-advertising-agencies/5889/)
- 87% of North American digital agencies have fewer than 50 employees. In one survey, 88 of 115 agencies had 1 to 15 employees. Promethean Research's 2026 sample averaged 31 employees and $4.43M revenue, which skews toward larger agencies [aggregator snippet] — [QuickSEO "State of SEO agency industry 2026"](https://quickseo.ai/blog/the-state-of-the-seo-agency-industry-in-2026-50-stats); [Haus Advisors, 2026](https://www.hausadvisors.com/blog/marketing-agency-industry-statistics)
- Client counts: 79% of agencies with 3 to 10 employees manage more than 20 clients, and 22% manage 100+ "at low retainers, with junior or templated delivery". About 70% keep each account manager under 10 clients [aggregator snippet, original survey unclear] — [QuickSEO 2026](https://quickseo.ai/blog/the-state-of-the-seo-agency-industry-in-2026-50-stats)
- SE Ranking's 2025 survey of 260 agencies: 94% serve small or local businesses and 64% charge less than $1,000/month [snippet; seranking.com blocked; vendor survey] — [SE Ranking SEO pricing survey 2025](https://seranking.com/blog/seo-pricing/), summarized in [roicool "3 surveys" 2026](https://www.roicool.com/en/blog/seo-pricing)
- Ahrefs' 2024 poll of 439 providers put local SEO at $1,557/month on average. GoodFirms (300+ agencies, 20+ countries) found 48% in the $1,500 to $5,000/month tier. The surveys disagree a lot — [roicool 2026](https://www.roicool.com/en/blog/seo-pricing); [GoodFirms](https://www.goodfirms.co/resources/seo-pricing-plans)
- "Most popular pricing range $500–$1,000/month" and "53% prefer monthly retainers" [snippet] — [SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/seo-pricing/)
- A claim that "56.2% of SEO agencies raised prices in 2025–2026, with AI search optimization work cited as the leading driver" [search-snippet synthesis; original source not verified, treat as unconfirmed] — [roicool 2026](https://www.roicool.com/en/blog/seo-pricing)

### Inferences
- Take the local-SEO/GBP-focused subset as maybe 10k to 30k US agencies and freelancers. That is my estimate: no source counts local-SEO agencies specifically.
- At $500 to $1,500/month per client, an agency can resell a $30 to $60 per-client audit ($149 for 5) at $200 to $500. The margin story works.
- The "100+ clients at low retainers" shops are the best fit: they need templated deliverables and cannot build AI-agent testing themselves.

### Gaps
- No reliable count of US agencies focused on local businesses, or on salons, med spas or dentists.
- Revenue per agency for the local-SMB segment was not found. The Promethean average skews to larger agencies.

## 2. Tool stack and spend; add-on buying; marketplaces

### Takeaway
Local agencies already pay for a stack of cheap per-location tools: BrightLocal, Whitespark, Local Falcon, GoHighLevel at $97 to $497, and Semrush. All of these incumbents have added AI-visibility tracking in 2025 to 2026. A typical small local agency plausibly spends $300 to $1,000+/month on tools. GoHighLevel and Vendasta have marketplaces that reach tens of thousands of agencies, but getting listed takes time, so they are not a 30-day channel.

### Cited Findings
- BrightLocal: 6,000+ SEO agencies use it (self-reported). Plans cost about $29 to $44/month per location (Track, Manage, Grow). It launched "AI Insights" [vendor] — [BrightLocal agencies page](https://www.brightlocal.com/agencies/); [BrightLocal AI Insights](https://www.brightlocal.com/blog/introducing-ai-insights/); [Localo 2026](https://localo.com/blog/best-local-seo-tools-for-agencies)
- Whitespark: Local Rank Tracker from about $29/month, Citation Finder about $79/month, plans $33 to $149/month — [Localo 2026](https://localo.com/blog/best-local-seo-tools-for-agencies)
- Local Falcon now tracks ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Grok, Copilot, AI Overviews and AI Mode with "Share of AI Voice". Every plan includes white-label reporting. It starts at $24.99 and runs to an Enterprise range of $499 to $4,999/month. It is credit-based, so agency usage adds up [vendor/aggregator] — [Local Falcon](https://www.localfalcon.com/); [citedindex review](https://citedindex.com/local-falcon); [Beamtrace](https://beamtrace.com/alternatives/local-falcon)
- Semrush AI Visibility Toolkit: $99/month per domain. For an agency, 5 clients comes to about $495 to $595/month and 10 clients to about $1,090/month. Semrush One bundles cost $199, $299 and $549/month [third-party reviews] — [Semrush AI pricing](https://www.semrush.com/pricing/ai/); [Trakkr](https://trakkr.ai/reviews/semrush-review/pricing)
- GoHighLevel: $97 (3 sub-accounts), $297 (Unlimited, white-label), $497 (SaaS Pro), plus $20 to $150/month in usage fees — [Ruzuku 2026](https://www.ruzuku.com/compare/gohighlevel-pricing); [HighLevel support](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000001156-highlevel-pricing-guide)
- The GHL Marketplace sells snapshots, custom apps and services from agencies and developers. Pricing can be free, one-time or recurring, and anyone with an agency account can list snapshots [third-party guide] — [gohighlevelgrowthstack 2026](https://gohighlevelgrowthstack.com/guides/gohighlevel-marketplace-2026)
- Vendasta Marketplace: 250+ products, reaching "66,000+ partners and 8.5M+ SMBs" (self-reported). Vendors apply to be listed for resale. ### Inferences
- A $149 trial is below the "ask my partner" threshold for most agency owners. $299/month is in the same range as GHL Unlimited or a Semrush One seat, so it has to beat a real incumbent line item.
- Agencies clearly adopt new add-on tools. The fast growth of Local Falcon, Semrush AI Toolkit and Ayzeo points that way, although no survey measures purchase speed.
- Marketplaces (GHL, Vendasta) are distribution for months 2 to 6, not days 1 to 30. Vendasta needs vendor approval, and the GHL marketplace needs an app build or a listed service.

### Gaps

## 3. AI-search demand: are clients asking, and are agencies selling AEO/GEO?

### Takeaway
Consumer demand signals are strong, and in 2026 Google AI Mode added **agentic booking and AI calling for beauty, home repair and pet care** in the US. That is a real, datable hook for "can an AI actually book your client?". Agencies are packaging "AI visibility audits" for local clients at $99 to $1,500 one-off and $500 to $1,500/month. I found no hard survey number on the share of local clients asking agencies about ChatGPT.

### Cited Findings
- BrightLocal LCRS 2026 (1,002 US consumers): 45% used AI tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity to find local businesses, up from 6% the year before. 88% verify before acting. Caution: a 6% to 45% jump in one year suggests a change in question wording [snippet; brightlocal.com blocked] — [BrightLocal LCRS 2026](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/); [Bill Hartzer](https://www.billhartzer.com/local-search/ai-local-business-recommendations-45-percent/)
- SOCi 2026 Local Visibility Index: ChatGPT recommends only 1.2% of local business locations, against 35.9% appearing in Google's 3-pack. The sample is brands with 50+ locations, so it does not represent single-location SMBs [vendor] — [SOCi LVI](https://www.soci.ai/insights/lvi/)
- Google I/O 2026: AI Mode passed 1B monthly users. Agentic booking now covers local services, and for home repair, beauty and pet care Google can call the business for the customer, "rolling out to everyone in the U.S. this summer". It started as a Labs experiment covering beauty/wellness appointments — [Google blog, I/O 2026](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/ai-story/googles-ai-mode-adds-agentic-booking-for-tickets); [Ignite Visibility](https://ignitevisibility.com/google-agentic-ai/)
- Whitespark 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors (47 experts, published Nov 2025) added an "AI search visibility" category for the first time. Its conclusion: local and AI signals have largely merged — [Whitespark LSRF 2026](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)
- BrightLocal agency survey: the top skill agency marketers want to develop is AI/ML (59%). The top client problems are "revenue not meeting expectations" (46%) and "customers cannot find the business online" (37%) [snippet; year of the edition unclear] — [BrightLocal agency survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-marketing-industry-survey-agencies/)
- Agency resale pricing: one-time AI visibility audits run $99 to $499 (entry), $500 to $1,500 (small local) and $2,500 to $5,000+ (multi-location). Agencies reselling AI visibility reporting charge $500 to $1,500 per client per month [vendor blogs] — [Rankley](https://www.rankley.com/blog/how-to-sell-ai-visibility-audits-to-local-clients); [Respona](https://respona.com/blog/white-label-ai-visibility-services/)
- AEO/GEO retainers: small-business packages start near $1,500/month and mid-market runs $2,000 to $8,000. Only a few GEO agencies publish prices [agency blogs] — [Digital Elevator](https://thedigitalelevator.com/blog/aeo-and-geo-pricing-guide/); [Derivatex](https://derivatex.agency/blog/geo-agencies-public-pricing-2026/); [DemandLocal](https://www.demandlocal.com/blog/how-to-price-geo-services/)

### Inferences
- The "bookability" angle (can an agent complete a booking, not just mention the business) differs from the crowded "AI mention tracking" category, and the Google AI Mode agentic-booking rollout gives it urgency for salons and home services specifically.
- Agencies want AI knowledge (59%) and want something to show clients. A done-for-you, white-label audit fits the "help me look smart about AI" need.

### Gaps
- No 2025 to 2026 survey found that quantifies "% of local SMB clients asking their agency about ChatGPT/AI". Reddit, Local Search Forum and Facebook threads did not show up in search.
- Adoption of Google agentic booking by actual SMBs (via booking partners) is not quantified.

## 4. Buying behavior and channels

### Takeaway
At a small local agency the buyer is almost always the owner/founder, which means fast decisions for under $300. They buy things that are white-label, cheap per client, easy to mark up, and that support retention and upsells. Inbox saturation is high and cold-email reply rates are falling. Warm channels (communities, marketplaces, content showing a real client audit) will outperform generic cold email.

### Cited Findings
- Agencies of 1 to 15 people dominate the population (see Q1), which implies owner-led buying — [QuickSEO 2026](https://quickseo.ai/blog/the-state-of-the-seo-agency-industry-in-2026-50-stats)
- White-label is standard across competitors: Local Falcon (all plans), Ayzeo (Pro $149, dashboard +$299), Rankley ($149 to $299 tiers), Vendasta ($499 for full white label) — [Local Falcon](https://www.localfalcon.com/); [Ayzeo pricing](https://ayzeo.com/pricing); [Rankley pricing](https://rankley.com/pricing/)
- Local Search Forum (run by Sterling Sky / Joy Hawkins) is the main local-SEO pro community. It has a free tier plus a $129/month private LocalU community, and a related Facebook group, "Local Search Pros" — [Local Search Forum](https://localsearchforum.com/); [BrightLocal community list](https://www.brightlocal.com/resources/top-local-seo-forums-communities/); [LocalU](https://localu.org/training/)
- Reseller marketplaces: Vendasta (66k+ partners, self-reported) and the GHL Marketplace — [Vendasta](https://www.vendasta.com/vendors/); [GHL guide](https://gohighlevelgrowthstack.com/guides/gohighlevel-marketplace-2026)

### Inferences
- Likely objections: "I already have Local Falcon/Semrush AI tracking", "is this just a ChatGPT prompt?", "will you poach my clients?" (white-label, no-contact policy needed), "can't verify results", and "a foreign solo founder, no track record".
- The best proof is a free, unsolicited mini-audit of one of the agency's own visible clients (from their portfolio or case studies) showing the AI failing to book. That is concrete and hard to ignore.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on how many cold pitches agency owners receive. Reddit r/agency threads were not surfaced.
- No data on community rules (LSF and Facebook groups often ban self-promotion).

## 5. Competition for AI-audit spend

### Takeaway
The "AI visibility tracking" layer is crowded and cheap: $25 to $150/month entry, often white-label. Players include Local Falcon, BrightLocal, Semrush, Ayzeo, Rankley, GrackerAI, SOCi and Birdeye. "Agent-readiness/bookability" is newer, with at least AgentHermes and Capconvert Cortex. A human-verified, done-for-you booking test remains a differentiator, but only while it stays clearly different from automated trackers.

### Cited Findings

## 6. Cold-outreach benchmarks

### Takeaway
Expect a 2 to 3% average reply rate on B2B cold email in 2026, 4 to 6% top decile, and 0.5 to 2% positive replies. Agencies are heavily pitched, so assume the low end unless the email carries a personalised proof point.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 average cold-email reply rate is 2 to 3%, down from 3 to 4% in 2024 because of inbox saturation and spam filtering. Top-decile campaigns reach 4 to 6%. Positive replies are 0.5 to 2%. Over 8% "usually tied to tight ICP, buying signals, or prior familiarity" [vendor/aggregator benchmarks] — [Instantly benchmark 2026](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026); [Apollo](https://www.apollo.io/insights/what-is-a-good-benchmark-for-reply-rates-in-cold-outreach); [Cleanlist, Feb 2026: 3.1%](https://www.cleanlist.ai/blog/2026-02-18-cold-email-response-rate-statistics)
- Segment-specific numbers for agency recipients exist at Puzzle Inbox but could not be read (site blocked) — [Puzzle Inbox](https://puzzleinbox.com/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks-2026-by-segment)

### Inferences (math, estimates)
- 1,000 well-targeted emails → about 20 to 30 replies → 5 to 20 positive → 3 to 8 calls or trials → at a 30 to 50% close on a $149 trial, 2 to 4 buyers (about $300 to $600). Reaching $1 to 5k in 30 days would need 3,000+ sends with good deliverability, plus several conversions to $299/month, or a higher-ticket offer. Personalised proof (auditing one of their real clients) could plausibly lift reply rates to 5 to 10%. That is unverified.

### Gaps
- No reliable meeting-rate or close-rate data for selling to agencies.

## Blunt conclusion (analyst judgment, based on the above)
- **Fit:** The segment is good for *someone*. It is large, fragmented, owner-led, used to paying $30 to $300/month per tool, hungry for AI talking points, and it has a real 2026 trigger in Google AI Mode agentic booking and calling for beauty and home services. For fast money it is only medium: agencies are the most over-pitched B2B audience, and cheap automated AI-visibility trackers already cover "are you mentioned in ChatGPT".
- **$1 to 5k in 30 days** is achievable at the low end ($1 to 2k) only with a sharp proof-led offer. $5k is a stretch for an unknown founder without an audience.
- **Offer that converts best (inference):** send a free, unsolicited "we tried to book [their client] via ChatGPT/AI Mode and here's what broke" 1-page teardown. Then a **$149 pack of 5 white-label audits** (or $197 for 10) that the agency resells at $300 to $500 each. Upsell to **$299/month** for about 10 audits/month plus monthly re-tests. An alternative that helps early revenue: $497 one-off "AI bookability sprint" for 10 to 15 clients, with a money-back guarantee.
- **Top 3 risks:** (1) Commoditization and confusion with $25 to $149/month automated AI-visibility tools, which caps price. (2) Cold-email fatigue among agencies (2 to 3% reply) plus low trust in an unknown, overseas solo founder. (3) Weak or noisy findings: AI assistant answers are non-deterministic and agentic booking coverage is still rolling out, so audits may be hard to reproduce and fixes hard to prove. That hurts retention past month 1.
